AI Search Optimization for Local Businesses: How to Get Recommended by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini

By Eric Camehl, Get Noticed AI• August 18, 2026• 8 min read

Consumer use of AI assistants for local recommendations went from 6% to 45% in one year. Almost no local business has adapted, which is precisely why the window is open.

The short answer

Local businesses earn AI search visibility by building an evidence trail that answer engines can read: presence on expert-curated "best of" lists, one clearly structured page per service with the answer stated in the first two sentences, a strong and recent review profile spread across multiple third-party platforms, consistent business data everywhere it appears, and structured data that states the facts unambiguously. In the 2026 ranking factor data, AI visibility is driven by on-page content (24%), reviews (16%), citations (13%), and links (13%), with the Google Business Profile contributing only about 12%.

The shift: 6% to 45% in a single year

BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 recorded one of the sharpest behavioral changes in the history of local search: the share of consumers using ChatGPT or other generative AI for local business recommendations rose from 6% to 45%, making AI the third most popular recommendation source behind Facebook and Google. Over the same period Google's share as a review source fell from 83% to 71%, and the average consumer now consults six different review sites when choosing a business.

The competitive picture in this new channel is unusual. SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index found that appearing in ChatGPT's local recommendations is roughly thirty times harder than ranking in Google's local results, and that fewer than half of the businesses leading Google local results also appear in AI local recommendations. In other words, winning Google does not transfer automatically, and the field is largely empty. SOCi also found only 68% of business contact information surfaced by ChatGPT and Perplexity matches the corresponding Google Business Profile details, a data-consistency problem that is also a first-mover opportunity.

What actually drives AI search visibility

The 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey scored 187 factors for AI search visibility for the first time, using input from 47 local search practitioners. Two things stand out. First, the weighting differs sharply from the map pack: on-page content carries about 24%, reviews 16%, citations 13%, links 13%, the Google Business Profile 12%, personalization 9%, social 9%, and behavioral signals the least. Second, the individual factors at the top are not the ones local SEOs traditionally prioritize.

Where AI assistants get local answers

Understanding the retrieval mix tells you where to invest. BrightLocal's analysis of ChatGPT Search for local queries found sources split roughly 58% business websites, 27% business mentions, and 15% online directories. Whitespark's study of AI Overviews in local search, 540 manual queries across Houston, Phoenix, and Denver in six industries, found AI Overviews appeared on 68% of local business-type queries on average, and that in a hybrid-intent plumber data set, 60% of the cited sources were third-party publishers such as Reddit, Quora, Yelp, Thumbtack, HomeGuide, Indeed, and ZipRecruiter, with only 40% pointing at individual local businesses.

The same study exposes an important nuance about intent. AI Overviews appeared on 92% of informational queries and 97% of hybrid queries, but only 15% of pure local-intent queries, where local packs appeared 93% of the time. Here's the practical point: your map pack work still owns "plumber near me," while your blog and service content own "how much does it cost to replace a water heater", and that informational surface is where AI citation is won. Ahrefs' analysis of 300,000 keywords found click-through rate drops an average of 34.5% when an AI Overview is present, so being cited inside the answer matters more each quarter.

How to structure content so it gets quoted

Answer engines extract passages, not pages. That changes how a page should be written.

  1. Lead with the answer. Open every section with two to three sentences that fully answer its heading, then expand. The 2026 survey's commentary on service page content makes the same point: clear and concise beats volume, give the reader the recipe at the start.
  2. Write headings as questions or explicit statements. "How much does AC replacement cost in Tampa?" is retrievable. "Our Approach" is not.
  3. Make every claim self-contained. Avoid "as mentioned above." A quoted paragraph must stand alone, because that is how it will appear.
  4. Put comparisons in tables. Pricing tiers, service comparisons, and specification lists are extracted cleanly from tables and mangled from prose.
  5. Add a genuine FAQ block to service pages. "Presence of FAQs on service pages" ranks 43rd for AI visibility, and FAQ blocks map directly onto how users prompt assistants.
  6. State concrete facts. Numbers, prices, timeframes, and named locations are what an assistant can repeat with confidence. Vague marketing language is unquotable.
  7. Keep the brand name near the topic. Proximity of the brand name to target phrases influences inclusion in AI Overviews, so name the business in the same paragraph as the service and city rather than only in the header.

The structured data that matters

Structured data does not make an assistant like you, but it removes ambiguity about who you are, and ambiguity is why assistants hedge or substitute a competitor. Five types cover almost every local business need. Organization and LocalBusiness (or a more specific subtype such as MedicalBusiness, HVACBusiness, or Dentist) establish the entity, its identity, contact details, and service area. Service describes each individual offering, ideally one per service page, with an areaServed and an offer. FAQPage marks up question-and-answer content in machine-readable form. Article or BlogPosting establishes authorship, publication date, and topical scope for editorial content.

Three implementation habits separate useful markup from decoration. Use stable @id values and reference them across your pages so every page describes the same entity rather than a new one each time. Use sameAs to link your entity to its profiles on other platforms, Google Business Profile, Facebook, LinkedIn, Yelp, industry associations, which is how an assistant reconciles scattered mentions into one business. And make sure the markup matches the visible page content exactly; contradictions between structured data and page text reduce trust rather than build it.

Off-site: the citation and mention layer

Because three of the top five AI visibility factors are citation factors, the off-site layer is not optional. There are four workstreams worth running in parallel.

Aggregators and core directories. Submit identical business data to the major US data aggregators, Foursquare, Data Axle, TransUnion Digital Business Profile (formerly Neustar Localeze), and the YP network, then to the general directories consumers still use. Foursquare matters disproportionately here because its location dataset powers a wide range of consumer applications and is used by ChatGPT. BrightLocal estimates manual citation building takes about twenty minutes per listing, which is why most businesses either batch it or outsource it.

Industry-relevant domains. Prominence on the specific domains where your vertical is discussed ranks third for AI visibility. For a dentist that means Healthgrades and the state dental association; for HVAC it means Angi, HomeAdvisor, BBB, and the manufacturer dealer locators for the brands you install. Dealer and association directories are frequently overlooked and often the easiest wins.

Best-of lists and unlinked mentions. The single highest-ranked AI factor is presence on expert-curated best-of lists. Pitch local publications running "best [service] in [city]" roundups, respond to journalist source requests, and sponsor or participate in community events that get written up. Note that unlinked brand mentions rank twelfth, so coverage without a hyperlink still counts, which makes local press and podcast appearances more valuable than their SEO metrics suggest.

Review diversity. The authority of third-party review sites ranks fifth and their diversity ranks eleventh. Since consumers now consult six review platforms on average, a review presence spread across Google, Facebook, Yelp, and two or three vertical platforms serves both audiences at once.

llms.txt: an honest assessment

You will be sold llms.txt as an AI ranking file. The evidence does not support that framing. An analysis of more than 500 million AI bot traffic events in 2026 found only 408 requests that targeted llms.txt directly, a study of 300,000 domains put adoption at 10.13%, and Google has stated on the record that it does not support the standard and has no plans to, with one Google search engineer comparing it to the discredited keywords meta tag.

What llms.txt does do is serve agentic tools, coding assistants and MCP-based agents fetch it routinely, and it costs almost nothing to publish. Here's the simple answer: ship a short, curated llms.txt because it is cheap and may matter later, but do not attribute AI visibility to it. Visibility comes from the on-page, review, and citation work above.

How to measure AI visibility

AI channels do not appear cleanly in traditional analytics, so measurement has to be constructed. Start with a fixed prompt set: ten to twenty questions a real customer would ask an assistant, covering "best [service] in [city]," "who should I call for [problem] near [neighborhood]," and "how much does [service] cost in [city]." Run them monthly in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Mode, and record whether the business is named, which sources are cited, and which competitors appear. That citation list is your outreach target list.

Then verify data accuracy by asking each assistant directly for your business's phone number, hours, and address, since only 68% of that information currently matches Google Business Profile data. Where it is wrong, trace the incorrect value back to the directory or aggregator propagating it and correct it at source. Finally, segment referral traffic from AI domains in analytics and watch server logs for GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and Google-Extended so you know whether you are being crawled at all. Confirm none of those user agents are blocked in robots.txt.

One caution worth keeping in view: 21% of ChatGPT users switch to Google to verify information after a local search, and users evaluate an average of 3.7 businesses with 39% clicking through to a business website. AI visibility is therefore an addition to local search work, not a replacement for it.

Frequently asked questions

What is AI search optimization for local businesses? +

AI search optimization, also called answer engine optimization or AEO, is the practice of structuring a business's web presence, reviews, and third-party citations so that AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google Gemini, and Google AI Overviews recommend the business when users ask for a local provider. In the 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey, AI search visibility is driven mainly by on-page content at about 24%, reviews at 16%, citations at 13%, and links at 13%, with the Google Business Profile contributing about 12%.

How do I get my business recommended by ChatGPT? +

Build the evidence trail AI assistants read. The top-ranked AI visibility factor in the 2026 survey is presence on expert-curated best-of lists, followed by having a dedicated page for each service and prominence on industry-relevant domains. Practically that means earning inclusion in genuine local roundups and directories, publishing one clearly structured page per service with the answer stated up front, maintaining a strong and recent review profile across multiple platforms, and keeping business details identical everywhere, since only 68% of business contact information on ChatGPT and Perplexity currently matches Google Business Profile details.

Is AI search visibility harder than ranking on Google? +

Yes. SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index found that appearing in ChatGPT local recommendations is roughly 30 times harder to achieve than ranking in Google local results, and that fewer than half of the businesses leading Google local results also appear in AI local recommendations. That gap is the opportunity, because businesses building AI visibility now face far less competition than in Google's local pack.

Does schema markup help with AI search visibility? +

More than it helps map pack rankings. In the 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey, website content marked up in schema ranks 44th for AI search visibility compared with 162nd for the local pack. Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage, and Article markup give answer engines unambiguous facts about who the business is, what it sells, where it operates, and what it charges.

Should local businesses publish an llms.txt file? +

It is cheap to ship but should not be expected to produce citations. Analysis of more than 500 million AI bot traffic events in 2026 found only 408 requests targeting llms.txt directly, adoption sits at roughly 10% of domains, and Google has publicly confirmed it does not support the standard. Agentic tools do fetch it, so publishing a short curated file is reasonable future-proofing, but on-page structure, reviews, and third-party citations are where AI visibility is actually earned.

Do AI Overviews reduce clicks to local business websites? +

They reduce clicks overall while shifting which queries matter. An Ahrefs study of 300,000 keywords found click-through rate falls by an average of 34.5% when an AI Overview is present. Whitespark's local study found AI Overviews appear on 68% of local business-type queries on average, including 92% of informational queries but only 15% of local-intent queries, which means local packs still dominate ready-to-buy searches while informational content is where AI citation is won.
