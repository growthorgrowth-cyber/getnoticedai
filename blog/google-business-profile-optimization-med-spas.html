Google Business Profile Optimization for Med Spas: The 2026 Playbook

By Eric Camehl, Get Noticed AI• August 18, 2026• 7 min read

Med spas compete in one of the most crowded local categories in the country, and almost all of that competition is decided inside a three-result map pack. This is the field-tested sequence for making a med spa profile the one Google trusts.

The short answer

To optimize a med spa Google Business Profile in 2026, set Medical spa as the primary category, add only the secondary categories you genuinely deliver, fill every predefined service Google offers for those categories, keep the physical address visible rather than hiding it as a service area business, publish real treatment-room photos monthly, and build a steady flow of reviews so the profile never falls below 20 reviews or a 4.0 star average. Category and proximity dominate the map pack; reviews and content structure dominate whether AI assistants recommend you.

Why med spas live or die in the map pack

A prospective client searching botox near me or laser hair removal [city] almost never scrolls. Backlinko's analysis of local queries found that roughly 42% of searchers click a map pack result, and BrightLocal's 2026 consumer research shows how compressed the decision window is: 75% of consumers decide which local business to use in under thirty minutes, 28% decide in under five minutes, and 72% look at three or fewer businesses before choosing. For an aesthetics practice with a $600 average ticket and a high repeat rate, the difference between position two and position four in the map pack is the difference between a full injector calendar and a quiet Tuesday.

Google itself describes local ranking as the product of three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. You cannot change a client's distance from your building, which means every practical lever available to a med spa is either a relevance lever (categories, services, descriptions, website content) or a prominence lever (reviews, citations, mentions, links). The profile is where both are declared.

Categories: the single highest-use field

In the 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey, in which 47 local search practitioners scored 187 factors, the primary Google Business Profile category ranks as the number one local pack factor, ahead of proximity of the address to the point of search. Nothing else you can edit in ten seconds moves rankings as much.

For a practice offering injectables, laser, and skin treatments under medical supervision, Medical spa is normally the correct primary category. Choosing Day spa instead pulls the profile into a massage and facial competitive set and suppresses it for the injectable and device queries that generate revenue. Conversely, a practice whose core identity is dermatology should not label itself a med spa simply because the term is fashionable, Google's guidelines require you to represent the business as it is consistently represented in the real world, and the guidelines explicitly instruct you to "choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business."

Two disciplines matter here. First, add secondary categories only for services you can actually document being delivered on site, because a category you cannot support is a spam signal rather than a ranking signal. Second, revisit the category list quarterly. Google adds and retires categories continuously, and a category that did not exist when the profile was created may now be the best available match for a device or treatment you have since added.

Services, treatments, and the keyword surface most spas ignore

Once categories are set, Google exposes a list of predefined services tied to those categories, plus the ability to add custom services with your own titles and 300-character descriptions. This is the most under-used field on a med spa profile, and the 2026 survey data explains why it should not be. "Presence of GBP predefined services" now ranks 22nd among local pack factors, "keywords in GBP custom service titles" ranks 30th, and "keywords in GBP services" jumped seventy-five positions in a single edition of the survey, one of the largest single-year moves ever recorded in that study.

The practical workflow is straightforward. Turn on every predefined service that genuinely applies, then add custom services for the branded and device-specific treatments your market searches by name: Botox, Dysport, Juvéderm, Sculptra, Kybella, Morpheus8, CoolSculpting, HydraFacial, microneedling with PRP, IPL photofacial, laser hair removal by body area, and any weight-management or hormone service you provide. Write the description as a plain explanation of what the client gets, how long the appointment takes, and who it suits. Do not repeat the city name five times; keyword stuffing in profile fields is documented in the same survey as both a negative ranking factor and a suspension trigger.

The business description deserves the same restraint. Use it to explain what the practice is, which credentials supervise treatment, and which neighborhoods it serves, written as a sentence a human would read aloud. Stuffed descriptions rank 21st among negative factors and 35th among suspension risks, a bad trade for a field that carries limited direct ranking weight.

Address, hours, and the SAB mistake that gets profiles suspended

Some med spas hide their address believing it protects privacy or widens their reach. It does the opposite. "Address is showing on GBP (not SAB)" ranks seventh among all local pack factors in the 2026 survey, and Google's own service-area documentation is unambiguous: a service-area business is one that does not serve customers at its address. A med spa treats clients on site, so the address belongs on the profile. Presenting a storefront as a service area business appears in the 2026 data both as a negative ranking factor and as a suspension risk.

Hours have quietly become a serious ranking input. "Business is open at the time of search" now ranks fifth among local pack factors, and "proper hours set on GBP" climbed sixty-four positions to twenty-first. For a med spa this argues for publishing genuinely accurate hours including the evening and Saturday blocks you actually staff, and for using special hours around holidays instead of leaving stale weekday hours in place. If your injector works until 7pm on Thursdays, that hour is a visibility asset that most competitors leave on the table.

Complete the rest of the profile with the same rigor: map pin placed precisely on the suite entrance rather than the parking lot, attributes set honestly (wheelchair accessible entrance, restroom, gender-neutral restroom, appointment required, online care), booking link connected, and the phone number identical to the one on your website. Google reports that customers are 2.7 times more likely to consider a business reputable when its profile is complete, and 70% more likely to visit.

Reviews: volume, recency, and HIPAA-safe replies

Reviews carry 20% of the weight in the local pack group in the 2026 survey, up from 16% in the previous edition, and they are the second strongest group for AI search visibility. For med spas the consumer thresholds are the part worth memorizing. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 found that 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, 68% require at least a 4.0 rating, 31% will only consider a business at 4.5 or above, and 74% look specifically for reviews written within the last three months. Volume without recency reads as a practice that peaked two years ago.

That makes review generation a permanent process rather than a campaign. Ask at the moment of visible result, the two-week follow-up after filler or the post-treatment photo review, using a Google review link or QR code, which is exactly the mechanism Google recommends. Never offer a discount, free unit, or loyalty credit in exchange for a review; incentivized reviews violate Google's fake and misleading content policy and can remove the review and endanger the profile.

Replies are where aesthetics practices get into trouble. A public reply that says "so glad your lip filler settled beautifully" confirms both that the person was a patient and what they were treated for. Keep public replies free of any clinical detail, then move specifics to a private channel:

Positive review reply: "Thank you for taking the time to share this. Our team's goal is that every guest feels informed and comfortable from consultation onward, and we're glad that came through. We look forward to seeing you again."

Critical review reply: "Thank you for the feedback, we take it seriously and we'd like to understand what happened. Please contact our practice manager at (000) 000-0000 or the email on our profile so we can look into this directly."

Vary the wording. Google's guidance asks for replies that are professional, short, meaningful rather than repetitive, and conversational rather than promotional, and BrightLocal found that generic templated responses actively put off 50% of consumers. Also note that 89% of consumers expect owners to respond and 81% expect a response within a week, so speed is part of the signal.

Photos, posts, and engagement signals

Two newer factors reward practices that treat the profile as a living asset. "Quantity of reviews with photos" ranks 44th and "photo and video quality" ranks 46th in the 2026 local pack list, while "quantity of engagement signals on GBP" moved up to 34th. Meanwhile, AI-generated photos and videos on a profile now appear among both the negative factors and the suspension risks. The instruction that follows is simple: publish real, well-lit photography of your actual space, equipment, and team on a monthly cadence, and do not fill the gallery with stock or synthetic imagery.

Google Posts should carry the same discipline. Use them for genuine seasonal offers, new device announcements, event nights, and staff introductions, written in plain language. Keyword-stuffed posts rank 38th among negative factors, so a post that reads like a human wrote it outperforms one engineered for a phrase.

The website work that GBP cannot replace

The profile is only half the system. In the local organic group, the highest-weighted factor for the third survey running is having a dedicated page for each service, followed by the geographic keyword relevance of your content. A single "Services" page listing fourteen treatments cannot compete with fourteen substantive pages, each explaining candidacy, process, downtime, pricing range, and results for one treatment in one market.

Keep the NAP block on your site byte-identical to the profile, since HTML NAP matching GBP NAP ranks 15th in the local pack list. Link your GBP to the most relevant landing page rather than defaulting to the homepage. And structure every page so the answer arrives before the narrative: scannable content structure across the entire website ranks tenth for AI search visibility while sitting at 128th for the map pack, which tells you precisely which channel rewards clean formatting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best primary category for a med spa on Google Business Profile? +

For most med spas the correct primary category is "Medical spa." Choose the single category that matches how the business is genuinely recognized in the real world, then add secondary categories such as Skin care clinic, Laser hair removal service, or Hair removal service for treatments you actually deliver. Primary category is the highest-weighted local pack factor in the 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey, and Google's guidelines instruct businesses to use the fewest categories needed to describe their core business.

How many Google reviews does a med spa need? +

Aim for a minimum of 20 reviews and then maintain a steady flow. BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 found that 47% of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, 68% require a rating of at least 4 stars, and 31% will only consider businesses rated 4.5 or higher. Recency matters as much as volume: 74% of consumers look for reviews written in the last three months.

Can a med spa reply to Google reviews without violating patient privacy? +

Yes, but replies must never confirm that a person was a patient or reference any treatment, diagnosis, or clinical detail. Keep public replies short, thank the reviewer for the feedback, state a general service commitment, and move specifics to a private channel. Google's review-reply guidance also advises protecting privacy and avoiding promotional language, and generic templated replies put off 50% of consumers, so vary the wording.

Should a med spa hide its address and list itself as a service area business? +

No. A med spa treats clients at a physical location, so it must display its address. In the 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey, "address is showing on GBP (not SAB)" ranks as the seventh strongest local pack factor, and incorrectly presenting a storefront as a service area business is documented as both a negative ranking factor and a suspension risk.

How long does med spa GBP optimization take to show results? +

Profile corrections such as categories, services, hours, and attributes can influence rankings within days to a few weeks, while review velocity and citation consistency compound over one to three months. Google states there is no way to request or pay for better local ranking, so gains come from sustained relevance, proximity, and prominence signals rather than a one-time fix.
